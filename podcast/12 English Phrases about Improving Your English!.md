## to make progress
- to move toward a goal or improve
- I am making progress in my English every day by practice.
- She has made significant progress in her vocabulary over the last few weeks.
- We are making good progress on our new project.
- Despite the rain and the bumpy dirt road, we managed to make progress toward our destination.
    - Manage to + 動詞原形: 強調克服困難的過程
    - How did you manage to finish the project on time?
    - We made progress. => 我們取得了進展。（平鋪直敘）
    - We were able to make progress. => 我們能夠取得進展。（強調有這個能力或客觀條件允許）
    - We managed to make progress. => 我們歷經千辛萬苦，最後還是設法取得了進展。（強調克服障礙後的成功）

## to get better at + V-ing / Noun
- to improve your ability to do something.
- I want to get better at speaking English with native speakers.
- She is practicing every day to get better at playing the piano.
- He is trying to get better at cooking so he can prepare his own meals.
- I hope to get better at time management this year.

## to get the hang of something / V-ing
- to begin to understand how to do something.
- 意思是「抓到……的訣竅」、「掌握……的要領」或「漸漸上手 / 熟練」
- Don't worry if it is confusing at first; you will get the hang of it soon.
- It took me a few days, but I finally got the hang of driving a manual car.
- She is just starting her new job and trying to get the hang of the software.
- Once you get the hang of using natural idioms, speaking English becomes much easier.
- I’m slowly getting the hang of ordering food in English.

## to have come a long way
- to have made a lot of progress
- She has come a long way in her English studies, going from a beginner to speaking fluently.
- Looking back at where he as an intern, he has come a long way to become the company manager.
- Mobile phone have come a long way over the past few decades; they used to be huge bricks and now they are powerful computers.

## be on track
- to follow your plan toward your goal.
- be on track for + N（朝著某目標/結果順利進行）
- be on track to + V（按進度即將做某事）
- Everything is going smoothly, and the project is on track for its release next month.
- If we keep up this pace, we will be on track to finish all tasks before Friday.
- She is on track with her savings goal for a new apartment.
- With his excellent grades, he is on track to graduate with honors this summer.
- We were delayed earlier, but now we are back on track.

## to take it to the next level
- to choose challenges that will help you improve beyond your current level.
- She took her English skills to the next level by enrolling in an advanced conversation class.
- With years of experience and dedicated effort, he is ready to take his career to the next level.
- The new technology will help our company take product quality to the next level.
- To take our sales to the next level, we need to implement a more targeted marketing strategy.
- Adding a special mix of spices really took the dish to the next level.
- After working with a personal trainer, she managed to take her fitness routine to the next level.

## to brush up on + N / V-ing
- to practice something you already know.
- 「溫習」、「複習」或「重新提升/改進（已一段時間沒用的技能或知識）」。
- I need to brush up on my English talk before travelling to America next month.
- She spent the weekend brushing up on the coding skills for the upcoming job interview. 
- He is brushing up on basic math concepts to prepare for the test.

## to keep at it
- to continue working even when something is difficult.
- 「堅持下去」、「繼續努力」、「不放棄」
- Don't give up on learning English; As long as you keep at it, you will see a great progress.
- Learning to play the guitar is difficult at first, but if you keep at it, it gets much easier.
- Exercising every day was tough in the begining, but you kept at it and eventually reached your goal.
- Writing a novel takes time, but he keeps at it for two hours every night.

## to stick with it
- to continue doing something and not quit.
- 「堅持下去」、「繼續努力不放棄」（通常指在面對困難、挑戰或需要長遠累積時，繼續堅持做某件事或保持某種習慣）。
- Learning a new language isn't easy at first, but if you stick with it, you will see huge progress.
- Mastering a complex skill takes time, just stick with it and don't give up.
- Sticking to a workout routine can be tough, but she stuck with it and achieved her target.
- We encountered several problems initially, but we decided to stick with our original strategy.

## to fall behind
- to make less progress or no progress.
- 「落後」、「跟不上進度」或「拖欠（帳款/付款）」。
- fall behind + N / V-ing（在某方面落後）
- fall behind on [payments/rent/bills]（拖欠付款/租金/帳單）
- fall behind with [work/studies]（工作/課業進度落後）
- fall behind in...：強調在某個範圍、學科、進度裡落後（例如：fall behind in your studies、fall behind in class）
- If you miss too many classes, you will easily fall behind in class.
- We need to work faster; otherwise, we might fall behind schedule.
- The runner tripped at the start of the race and fell behind the other athletes.
- Company that fail to innovate will soon fall behind their competitors.
- He lost his job last month and has fallen behind on his rent.

## to catch up
- to reach the level you should be at after falling behind.
- 「趕上/追上（進度或前人）」、「補做（落後的事物）」以及「（朋友間）敘舊、聊聊近況」
- catch up with / on + N
- catch up to [someone]
- I spent all weekend catching up on my emails and coursework after being sick for a few days.
- She decided to stay home this weekend to catch up on sleep.
  - to stay up late
  - to stay up all night
- Walk ahead; I'll catch up with you in a few minutes.
- The small company is innovating rapidly to catch up to its primary competitors.
- We haven't seen each other for years, so let's grab a coffee and catch up.
- It was great to catch up with you after all this time.

## to see result
- to notice that your effort is working.
